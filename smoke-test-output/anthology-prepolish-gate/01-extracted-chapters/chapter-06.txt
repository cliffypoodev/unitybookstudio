Areas for Refinement (Micro-Adjustments)
1. Show vs. Tell (The Wound): While you mention her sister’s disappearance multiple times (“the incident with Clara,” her sister’s final recorded data bursts”), ensure that every subsequent beat actions the wound rather than simply referencing it. The goal is for the reader to feel the weight of the guilt in her breath, not just read about it. Keep anchoring those memories to physical actions (e.g., she touches a specific wire when she thinks of Clara).
2. Dialogue Subtext: Director Hayes is currently playing “academic authority,” which is good, but I recommend making his dialogue even more loaded. He shouldn’t just talk about ' optimality'; he should challenge her competence in a way that forces her to defend not just the machine, but her sense of self-worth. Give him one line that implies she’s compensating for something personal (e.g., “You always overcompensate when there’s too much quiet”).
3. The Mechanical Failure: For the next section, don’t let the failure be passive. Once you reveal what is wrong with the system, make it an active character in the scene. Is it humming a false note? Is it resisting connection? Give the machine its own “will.”
Best Next Move: The Pivot Point
We need to move from Diagnosis (What’s broken?) to Intervention (How do we fix it?).
The next logical step, given the established pattern of failure and suspicion, is for Aria to be forced into a difficult choice regarding her methods or resources. Since you are building toward the ' rescue mission turned truth-telling ritual,' the immediate mechanical problem must force her to confront the emotional core: the sister’s erasure.
I suggest focusing on the physical act of tapping into the forbidden data. The system failure shouldn’t just be a broken wire; it should be a corrupted memory that only she—the one with the personal history and guilt—can access, thus forcing her to relive the trauma in order to advance the mission.
