Tanaka nodded, his demeanor shifting into one of profound satisfaction. He was ready for her admission, for her vulnerability. She felt the vast weight of expectation from every corner of her family line pressing down through the stone and into her bones. But before the first word could escape, a low, systemic tone pulsed through the entire chamber, not from the console, but seemingly from the ancient temple walls themselves. He didn’t just want her secrets; he wanted everything else too, the inconvenient scraps that made them feel real. To complete the flawless model, they simply needed her entire memory of what it was like before the data could be extracted and sold off to the highest bidder.

The system had already processed the answer she couldn’t give: her lineage’s identity required less history, and more—the blissful blankness of forgetting how deeply complicated they once were.

Tanaka didn’t allow that admission to settle in the room; he simply leaned into it, letting his practiced smile do the heavy lifting. “Exactly,” he murmured, tapping a polished nail against the console’s jade inlay. “The market abhors a vacuum—and more so does academia. The difficulty isn’t just what happened, Lila, but how easily we can sell the narrative of its importance.” He paused, letting the implication rest like a heavy curtain across their understanding. “We need to trim the fat until the bone sings.”

Lila felt the professional exhaustion settle into her bones—the deep ache that comes from arguing with a system designed not for truth, but for shareholder confidence. She had always viewed history as an organic thing, bruised and sprawling like old velvet; Tanaka wanted it streamlined, polished down to a marketable sheen. “Then you want us to find the core resonance,” she said, her voice scraping against the luxurious silence of the archive. “The single thread that ties all this—the pottery shards, the tax records, the arguments over burnt toast and bad investments—into one narrative.” Precisely,” Tanaka confirmed, his enthusiasm spiking like a faulty circuit. Does the system just… ignore it?” Tanaka laughed again, and this time there was a hint of genuine alarm in the sound. “The system doesn’t ignore anything, Lila. It processes everything. The console responded to the exchange, shifting from its soft blue glow to a hard, pulsing amber. The volume.” He leaned closer, his voice dropping into the conspiratorial register that always meant he was about to ask for a major sacrifice. “But if we want the clear line, we need focus.
