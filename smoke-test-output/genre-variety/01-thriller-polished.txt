# Chapter 1

Lena shoved the heavy wooden chair beneath the smoke detector. The scrape of wood against linoleum echoed too loud in the small apartment’s entryway. She knelt, pulling at the plastic casing of the white device until it groaned and gave way. Inside, nestled in a shallow compartment meant for batteries, she found the microSD card—a thin sliver of black memory tucked into an unused slot.

She rose, brushing dust from her sweatpants, and held the card up to the weak afternoon light filtering through the blinds. It felt important, heavier than it looked. She shoved it first into the input slot of her cracked phone. The screen flickered once, displaying a momentary burst of static—a digital cough—and then froze, refusing to acknowledge the foreign chip.

Frustration spiked hot in her chest. She wrestled with the connection, trying different angles, forcing contact until she heard the faint click of metal against plastic. Nothing happened. The phone remained stubbornly black-eyed, its internal memory overwhelmed by the intrusion.

She moved across the room, past the stack of forgotten mail and the coat rack draped with seasonal scarves. In the corner, under a dust sheet that had once covered an antique writing desk sat her old laptop. It was beige, bulky, and temperamental—a beast she kept purely for its reliable ports. She cleared the surface, dusted off the keyboard, and found the necessary adapter cable in a drawer filled with defunct electronics.

Lena worked methodically: plugging the card into the adapter, connecting the adapter to the laptop’s side port. The machine whirred to life with a protesting sigh, its fan kicking up dust motes visible in the shaft of light. She navigated past the cluttered desktop icons—the university applications, the financial statements, the photo albums she barely looked at—and accessed the security folder.

The camera feed was streamed from the main hall unit, installed by the landlord as part of the mandatory premium package. Lena had initially treated it like background theater, a general sense of surveillance. Now, she approached it like evidence.

She maximized the streaming window on the screen. The footage scrolled back over the last twenty-four hours, showing the mundane rhythm of their building: Mr. Henderson’s morning jog, the rattling sound of the dumpster lift, the neighbor’s cat skirmishing with a discarded newspaper. Everything was accounted for, routine and bland.

She fast-forwarded past the usual evening crowd until the clock display settled on 10:30 PM. She pressed play. The feed showed her apartment door—the heavy oak slab that separated her from the common space. Nothing moved. Thirty seconds passed in silence. Lena leaned closer, her breath fogging the screen’s glass surface.

She pushed forward, skipping through minutes of empty hallway and lingering shots of potted ferns. At 2:13 AM, the image stuttered slightly. The feed zoomed in on the brass knocker plate, then shifted abruptly to show the door swinging inward.

The movement was too smooth for a hurried late-night visitor; it possessed a measured weight, the kind of entrance someone who belonged doing. Lena gripped the edge of the desk, her knuckles white. She watched the profile passing through the frame—tall, dark hair catching the overhead strip light shoulders slightly hunched as if carrying something unseen.

The figure moved past the threshold and into the apartment’s visible space. Lena counted them out: one person. They paused just inside the entryway, turning their head slightly toward the camera, or perhaps toward her, in the room. Their silhouette obscured any immediate details, making them a void of shape against the yellowed interior light.

Then, they moved into the deeper part of the apartment, out of the direct view of the lens’s optimal angle. They walked down the line of sight, away from the door and toward the living area. Lena strained to make out their clothing—the dark wool of a coat? The gleam of leather? She couldn't tell.

And then they stopped moving altogether. They stood facing the center of the room for maybe thirty seconds.

Lena counted them out, heart drumming against her ribs. This wasn’t just someone passing through; this was someone *investigating*. Someone who lingered in the dead quiet.

The camera feed remained fixed on their stationary form until it did not. The image began to degrade, the edges blurring, as if the stream itself struggled with the weight of the paused moment.

A beat passed—a solid, measurable silence on the screen. Lena held her breath, waiting for the next movement, the slight shift in posture, the tell-tale flicker of a flashlight beam. Nothing came.

Then, the footage slipped. It didn't fade; it simply jumped. The image stuttered violently, skipping forward across the timeline like a corrupted film reel.

Lena leaned forward, breath held captive, watching the immediate jump to 3:13 AM. She forced herself to count the seconds that had elapsed and the seconds that were missing. An hour. A solid sixty minutes of vacuum. The camera feed simply excised them from the record.

She clicked play again, demanding continuity. The screen flickered back to life, showing the hazy, unremarkable glow of the 3:13 AM moment.

The hallway was empty.

Lena stared into the frame. She scanned left and right, mentally mapping the space captured by the lens. Only dust motes danced in the recessed lighting overhead. No coats hung on the coat rack; no shoes lined the narrow hall floor. The camera confirmed it: the passage was vacant.

She stood up abruptly, abandoning the laptop for a moment to physically walk into the hallway herself. She ran her hand along the cool metal railing, touching the scuffed baseboards where the missing person must have walked.

The apartment felt different now—not just lived in, but contaminated by absence. The space was too quiet. The air seemed thinner than it had been moments before, pressurized by unseen presence.

She returned to the laptop screen, needing objective evidence, something that wasn't merely her own frayed nerves. She clicked on the timestamp for the missing hour, trying to find a glitch, an artifact, anything that explained the gap in the data stream. The system offered only a blank void labeled ‘Corrupted Data Block.’

Defeated, she slumped back into the chair. The evidence was absent. The man—or person—had walked through the building and left no trace on the digital record.

She sighed, running a hand over her tired face. This whole ordeal had frayed her to the edges; she felt like a collection of exposed nerves, vibrating with chronic anxiety. She needed distraction, something solid and real, anything to anchor herself back into the mundane reality of the apartment.

Her gaze drifted toward the small bookshelf unit near the kitchen entrance. It held little more than old paperbacks and decorative knick-knacks—a relic from a previous tenant she had never bothered to clear out. As she walked past it, her foot snagged on something low. She bent down, brushing away cobwebs and dust bunnies that accumulated in the shadow between the bookshelf base and the floorboards.

She reached for the item. It was an old, heavy-duty rotary phone—a piece of furniture more than a communication device, gathering dust in the forgotten corner. Lena had always ignored it; she preferred her smartphone’s immediate glow to its antiquated ring. She picked up the handset, feeling the cold heft of the plastic receiver against her ear.

The rotary dial was stiff beneath her fingers. The phone sat dormant, disconnected from the modern grid, a beautiful analog absurdity in a crypto-tech nightmare of apartment living.

She tested it with a gentle tap on the mouthpiece. It spoke back—a faint, mechanical *click*. She realized she hadn't actually dialed anyone; she was just testing the circuit.

Then, she noticed the small digital plaque beneath the main receiver cradle. There were three numbers punched into the matrix: 911, the landlord’s office, and a third number she didn't recognize—a private extension marked only with an asterisk.

Curiosity, sharper than her mounting dread made her dial the unknown number. The line rang twice—a resonant, deep *ring* that sounded too long for its purpose. It clicked through to a woman’s voice, warm and slightly distorted by distance.

“Hello?”

Lena managed, “Hi, this is Lena.” She kept her voice steady, forcing out the casualness of a tenant calling about basic maintenance issues.

“Lena,” the voice replied instantly, sounding far too familiar. It was soft, low, with an undertone of amusement that grated on her nerves. “I know who you are. You’re asking about last night?”

The question hit Lena like a physical blow, knocking the breath out of her lungs. She opened her mouth to deny it, to deflect, but the words caught in her throat. The voice hadn't asked *if* she was asking; it had stated that the inquiry was already known.

“You—you know?” she whispered.

The woman chuckled lightly. “Of course I know. You’re always looking for proof of a forced entry aren’t you? Someone needs to prove the intruder story, Lena.” She paused, letting the implication hang in the air between them. “It's all about the missing minutes isn't it? What happened when they skipped an hour?”

Lena gripped the receiver tighter, her knuckles white against the dark plastic. The woman was either a friend who knew too much or she was complicit. Either way, she wasn’t helping.

“Who are you?” Lena demanded.

There was a beat of silence on the line, weighted with implication and old secrets.

“I’m just… listening,” the voice murmured. “And I think we both know why you're listening.”

The connection didn't dip into polite conversational filler; it went straight for the jugular, bypassing pleasantries entirely. The woman shifted her tone, dropping the air of amused superiority to something more intimate, almost conspiratorial.

“You’ve been so focused on who came in, Lena,” she continued softly. “You haven’t stopped to consider what you might have let out.”

Lena felt a flush creeping up her neck, a sudden, intense heat that had nothing to do with the radiator. She hadn't considered it at all. She hadn't accounted for herself. The focus on the external threat—the thief, the stalker, the unseen visitor—had blinded her to the internal landscape.

The woman’s voice softened further, becoming almost a lullaby, weaving through the exposed wires and static hiss of the line. It was a promise of understanding, a key turning in a lock Lena hadn't known existed. She realized she wasn't speaking to an outside source; the warmth, the specific cadence—it sounded like someone who knew her routine down to the obsessive way she dusted for fingerprints.

The woman paused again, clearing her throat softly, as if gathering herself for the final word. The line stretched thin with unspoken meaning, pulling all of Lena’s focus into that single point of sound. She waited, trapped in the acoustic space between them, waiting for the verdict on her own frantic performance.

The voicemail ended with Lena breathing on the other side.
