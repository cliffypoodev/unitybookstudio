# Chapter 1

Aaron pressed his ear against the plaster of the kitchen wall, pushing hard enough to feel the slight unevenness of the decades-old paint beneath his knuckles. He closed his eyes, shutting out the dusty glare spilling in from the bay window, and listened until the scraping sound faded into a dull thrum—the constant, underlying heartbeat of the house itself.

Nothing. Just the echo of his own pulse hammering against the drywall.

He moved his head slightly left, angling himself toward the built-in pantry unit. He leaned back in, pressing again. The silence was immediate, oppressive, sucking the air out of him. He waited for the *thump*, the quick, internal rapping sound that had become the soundtrack to his life here—the source of their dread, the unseen occupant living just behind the veneer of suburban decay.

He opened his eyes and stared at the spot where the sound seemed strongest, slightly above a chipped floral tile border. The knocking wasn't random; it was rhythmic, almost deliberate, like someone tapping out Morse code in distress. It moved—he’d noticed that since last night—whenever he shifted his focus, always staying just out of direct sightline.

“It moves when you look away,” Mara had said yesterday, her voice thin and tired, “Like it’s playing hide-and-seek with your hearing.”

Aaron dismissed her theory; this was something physical. He knelt down, brushing aside the dust motes that danced in the sunbeam, and pulled a roll of blue painter's tape from his pocket. He cleaned the area with an old rag, then pressed the sticky edge of the blue tape firmly against the wall, marking the spot where the knocking was loudest—or at least, where he *thought* it was loudest.

He stood up, dusting off his knees, and retreated a few steps into the narrow hall leading to the dining room, letting the space between him and the source build its own charged tension. He watched the tape, which seemed dull and meaningless against the faded sage paint, waiting for the sound to reassert itself.

It came again, softer this time, sounding more like something being dragged than tapped. *Scratch… tap… scratch.* It was coming from lower down, near where the electrical conduit ran into the kitchen counter backsplash.

“You're going to wear a groove in that plaster,” Mara called out from the center of the room, her voice drifting over the clatter of dishes she was stacking. She didn't look up; her attention was clearly focused on some spreadsheet or listing sheet—the transactional reality of their lives overriding the ghostly one.

“It’s nothing, just a draft,” Aaron lied smoothly, running his hand along the cool surface of the counter.

Mara finally straightened, pushing herself off the island and crossing her arms over her chest. She looked at him, not with concern, but with an exhausted sort of resignation—the look one gives a brother who is predictably dramatic.

“It’s more than a draft,” she said simply. “You know it's been doing that since Mom died.”

He bristled. “And you think the answer is just 'time passing'?”

“I think the answer is exhausting yourself trying to force it into some neat little explanation for the listing agent,” Mara countered, her voice rising slightly. She gestured vaguely toward the front door, where the late afternoon sun was already beginning its descent. “We have a showing in twenty minutes.”

Twenty minutes. The deadline suddenly felt like an impossibly short amount of time to dismantle years of accumulated psychic dread and structural decay.

Aaron swallowed, his mouth suddenly dry. He looked back at the blue tape marking the source of the noise, then glanced at Mara's tense posture, her neat clothes betraying a deep, underlying anxiety about money more than ghosts.

“Alright,” he said, letting the moment’s tension drain into sheer willpower. “I need to know what we're dealing with.”

He walked back toward the kitchen wall, ignoring Mara’s warning glances. He picked up the utility knife—the heavy, sharp blade that had been sitting on the utility hook near the sink—and returned to the section marked by the blue tape. The paint chipped easily under the pressure of the steel.

*Scrape.*

The initial cut was slow, a careful incision into the aged plasterboard. Dust puffed out in a dry cloud, smelling of pulverized gypsum and mildewed time. He worked methodically, following the line of the wall joint until he hit the lathe and the brick behind it. The sound of the metal singing against solid material was satisfyingly definitive.

He widened the slit with his hands, pulling back the crumbling plaster strips like heavy curtains. Beneath the surface layer, he revealed a network of exposed lath, brittle and yellowed by age, running across the width of the wall. He cleared away chunks of drywall dust until his hand could fit into the newly created cavity.

He leaned in, ignoring the grit falling onto his shoulder, pressing his ear right against the rough opening. The immediate sound was—silence. Deep, encompassing silence. No frantic tapping, no dragging scrape. Just empty space and the echo chamber effect of the house itself.

He waited a beat, counting to three. Nothing. He shifted his weight, brushing dust off his jeans.

“Well?” Mara prompted from across the room, suddenly invested. She abandoned her stack of dishes and was now watching him like he held the key to their financial solvency.

Aaron reached into the cavity, running his hand along the rough edges of the framing. His fingers grazed something small, something irregular that wasn't wood or plaster—it snagged on his nailbed. He tugged slightly. It slid free with a faint *clink*.

He pulled it out and held it in his palm.

It was a child’s bracelet. Not just any bracelet; it was intricate made of tarnished silver linked to miniature turquoise beads, the kind that had been popular at least thirty years ago. It looked heavy, weighted by memory. He flipped it over, examining the tiny engraving on the clasp: *Lila*.

He didn't know who Lila was, or why she belonged in a cavity wall, but the object felt profoundly settled into the space, as if it had been waiting specifically for him to find it. It was too clean, too cared for, to be accidental junk.

Aaron let go of the bracelet, letting it fall lightly into his open hand. He stood up straight, brushing dust from his jacket sleeves, and moved away from the exposed section of wall. The dramatic reveal had momentarily broken the tension.

He took a deep breath settling his gaze on the newly compromised stud—the visible crossbeam that held the structure together. He raised his fist, not in question or fear, but with firm intention.

*Knock.*

The sound was dull and solid, resonating deep within the frame. It was the sound of wood meeting wood, a foundational resonance that seemed to echo beyond the walls themselves.

He waited for any secondary noise—a drip, a scrape, a whispering breath. There was nothing but the fading reverb of his own impact. He dropped his hand, slightly breathless. The house sagged around him, releasing its decades of held-back sound into the quiet room.

A sudden, sharp beat of sound came from deep within the cavity he had just exposed—a rhythm that seemed to come not from the stud itself, but from something nestled deeper, behind the wood grain, and it was accompanied by a faint metallic *jangle*. It wasn't the quick rap of distress; it was a slow, measured beat, like someone tapping out an intentional, steady pulse.

Aaron stared at the exposed gap in the wall, his breath catching in his throat. The knocking had started again, but this time, it was deeper than the surface noise he’d been chasing—it was coming from something *inside* the source of the structure itself.

***

The hand inside the wall knocked back.
